# Storyline guide

The brief should read like a well-built explanation, not a pile of findings. Knowledge is constructed and situated: the reader starts from where they are and is walked, step by step, to what they asked about.

## Structure

```
# Title
> Mode: quick|deep · Date · N sources

## TL;DR
5 lines: the whole story, compressed.

## The map
How the brief unfolds: the path from the big picture to the specific question (one short paragraph or list).

## 1. The big picture
What this field/problem is, why it exists, what it is for.

## 2. Foundations
Concepts the reader needs before the main topic, including ones they didn't ask for. One short subsection each. Start from the reader's level.

## 3. How it works
The core mechanics/ideas, from simple to detailed.

## 4. The specific question
What the user actually asked, now that the foundations are in place.

## 5. State of the art and open questions
Where the field is, where sources disagree, what is unresolved. (deep: grounded in papers)

## 6. Where to go next
An ordered reading path: what to read first, and why each item.

## Glossary
Terms and the exact vocabulary to search for more.

## References
```

Adapt the sections to the topic: merge, rename or drop sections when it makes the story clearer. The order must still go from broad to specific.

## Writing rules

- Each section opens with the question it answers and ends with a bridge to the next one.
- Define a term at first use. Use concrete examples, numbers, or analogies for hard ideas.
- Use a table or a small diagram when it clarifies (comparisons, pipelines), not as decoration.
- Separate what the sources say (cited) from your own interpretation (say so).
- If you state something from your own knowledge without a source, say so.
- Be honest about uncertainty and disagreement between sources.
- Length follows the need: roughly 1,500-3,000 words for `quick`, 4,000-8,000 for `deep`. Don't pad.

## References

- Cite inline with numbers: `[1]`, `[2]`, `[1, 3]`.
- The final list contains only sources you actually opened.
- Format: `[n] Authors. Title. Venue or site, Year. URL or arXiv:ID`
- Add `(abstract only)` to papers you didn't read in full.
- Never invent or reconstruct a reference from memory.
